A SoftenTap guide

Understand your water before you buy a treatment system.

Hard water, softeners, salt-free conditioners, under-sink reverse osmosis, and replacement filters are often sold as if they do the same job. They don’t. These guides show how to read a utility hardness report and what each type of system actually does — with sources, and without pretend lab results.

Start with these three guides

Guide 1

How to read a hardness report

Find total hardness on your utility’s Consumer Confidence Report, convert ppm or mg/L to grains per gallon, and use WaterOne (Leawood and Johnson County, Kansas) as a worked example: 135 ppm is about 7.9 grains per gallon.

Guide 2

Softener vs conditioner vs filter vs RO

A salt-based softener removes hardness. A salt-free conditioner does not. A carbon filter and an under-sink reverse osmosis unit solve different problems again. Here’s the honest split, including certifications to look for.

Read the guide

Guide 3

Do you need a softener at about 8 gpg?

On the USGS scale, about 8 grains per gallon is hard water — not very hard. For many city supplies, including WaterOne’s, a softener is a comfort and maintenance choice, not a safety requirement.
